How this plan works

Your run paces

Bike and run heart-rate zones

Zones follow Joe Friel’s percentages of lactate threshold heart rate (LTHR), found with a 30-minute solo time trial: your LTHR is the average heart rate over the last 20 minutes. Until you test, the plan describes effort in words.

Swim paces

Critical swim speed (CSS) per 100 m = (400 m time − 200 m time) ÷ 2. Aerobic swimming is CSS plus 8–15 seconds per 100 m; threshold repeats of 100–200 m are CSS plus 1–2 seconds. If you can’t yet swim 100 m without stopping, swim 25s or 50s with rest and still count the distance.

How the season is built

Most training is easy. Each sport has one harder session a week at most, following research showing well-trained endurance athletes do roughly 80% of their training at low intensity. Every fourth week is a recovery week (weeks 6, 10, 14, 18 and 22), which is standard coaching practice rather than a single study’s finding. Weekly load rises gradually from your recent 3–4 hours, in line with Gabbett’s guidance to keep this week’s load within about 0.8–1.3 times your recent four-week average. The final two weeks cut volume sharply while keeping some intensity, the taper pattern a 2007 meta-analysis found most effective.

Strength: bench progresses all season. Squat and deadlift push for gains through January, then hold steady during the biggest endurance weeks, because combined training can hold back lower-body strength in trained lifters. Heavy leg sessions sit early in the week, away from the weekend long ride and long run. Friday is gym-only, your endurance rest day. No gym on Sundays.

The block and set structure, recovery-week placement and gym timing are coaching judgment. Adjust any session if you’re ill, injured or unusually tired, and get a medical check-up before the training load rises.
